Reading the numbers

Round 1 WR (35.9%) edges Round 1 RB (34.2%) in win rate, and both post nearly identical loss rates (~19.5%) — the raw weekly outcome odds are a wash between the two positions at the top of drafts.
Durability is the real split. Round-1 RBs average 13.6 games played vs. 14.4 for Round-1 WR — and RB WAR (Durability) averages −1.15 at that tier vs. −0.69 for WR, the widest gap of any comparison in this dataset.
TE is the most top-heavy position. Round 2-3 TEs (mostly elite juniors like early Kelce/Andrews-tier ADP) post the best average WAR (+1.26) of any position/tier combination, despite a tiny sample (n=18).
QB loses gracefully. QB has the flattest win-rate decay by round and the lowest loss rates at every tier below Round 1 — streaming a QB is a materially safer bet than streaming an RB/WR/TE.
Depth-of-position matters. RB and WR both cross 50% loss rate by Round 11+; QB doesn't cross it until UDFA. Bench-depth RBs and WRs are structurally more likely to be losses than a Round 11+ QB streamer.
